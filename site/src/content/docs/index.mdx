## Why Health Relay?

Apple Health has no cloud API. The only way your data leaves the phone is
through an app on the phone, and the existing options pre-aggregate it,
change their payload between versions, or sync when they feel like it.
Health Relay does one job and does it exactly.

<CardGrid stagger>
  <Card title="Exact, incremental sync" icon="approve-check">
    Bookmarks on every Health type find exactly the days that changed. Each one is sent whole,
    every type and source, so your receiver ends up with what the phone has, edits and deletions
    included.
  </Card>
  <Card title="One open schema" icon="document">
    A single documented JSON format in Apple Health's own terms, versioned by name. If you read
    `export.xml` today, you already know it. [Read the schema](/reference/schema/).
  </Card>
  <Card title="Destinations you choose" icon="random">
    Add any HTTP endpoint with an optional bearer token and headers: your server, a home lab
    dashboard, a webhook. Every destination gets every day; one failing never holds up another.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Private by design" icon="seti:lock">
    No account, no backend, no analytics. Data goes only to your destinations, tokens live in the
    Keychain, and nothing is read that the schema doesn't name.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Reliable in the background" icon="rocket">
    Wakes when Health records something, catches up every morning, keeps unsent batches on disk
    and retries with backoff. It tells you plainly when iOS is in the way.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Per-source detail" icon="list-format">
    Daily statistics per source (Watch, iPhone, scale, apps) plus the raw samples where they
    matter: sleep stages, blood pressure, workouts with routes, ECG traces.
  </Card>
</CardGrid>

## How it works

<div class="flow">
  <div><strong>1. Health changes</strong>A watch sync, a weigh-in, last night's sleep.</div>
  <div><strong>2. Days marked</strong>The days that gained samples, plus today and yesterday.</div>
  <div><strong>3. Read whole</strong>Every type and source for each of those days.</div>
  <div><strong>4. Delivered</strong>JSON batches to each destination, kept until accepted.</div>
</div>

Write a receiver in an afternoon: accept a `POST`, check a token, store the
days, answer `200`. [Writing a receiver](/reference/receivers/) has the
rules, and the repository includes a reference receiver and canonical
fixtures to test against.

## See what arrives

Every destination keeps a log of its deliveries with the receiver's answer,
and **Data by type** shows which Health types had data in the last 30 days,
so a permission switched off in Settings is easy to spot.
